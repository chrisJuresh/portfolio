What changed in the tween

The first version sampled both outlines at equal arc length and joined mismatched contours with a slit. That is why it looked wrong, and it looked wrong in three specific ways.

  • Corners now match corners. Equal-arc sampling lands the corner of an E's arm against the middle of a curve, so every corner rounds off on the way across and the straight edges wobble. Corners are now found on both outlines, matched by cyclic dynamic programming, and points distributed between matched corners. A stem stays straight because both its ends are pinned.
  • The bowl closes instead of unzipping. Friz Quadrata's P and R have no counter — the bowl tapers to a point and never reaches the stem. The old version slit the sans counter open and joined it to its outer, which read as a zip being pulled. Now the bay is closed off with a chord across its mouth: the outer swallows the bay and the bay becomes a hole bounded by the same chord. The two chords coincide, so at rest it is Friz's own shape — and the tween's job becomes closing the mouth, which is what someone would draw.
  • Both ends are the typeface. At rest the page shows the real Bézier outlines, not a polygon of them. The tween runs only in between, and the handover is measured at about 0.2% of a pixel's worth of difference, so nothing jumps.

Finding that chord needs one more thing to be told to it: a letter has several places where its outline nearly touches itself across white, and the notch under a P's foot is a narrower gap than the mouth of its bowl. Picking the closest pair finds the serif every time. Picking the closest pair whose bay contains the counter finds the bowl.

If you pick one

  • Licensing splits the list. The Fontshare faces (Satoshi, Switzer, General Sans, Cabinet Grotesk, Clash Display, Supreme, Ranade, Nippo, Chillax, Technor, Bespoke Sans, Pally) are free for commercial use but are not OFL — check the terms before anything ships. The rest are OFL from google/fonts, same footing as Vollkorn and Spectral already in /fonts.
  • Nothing goes on the wire either way. Both ends bake to paths, exactly as Friz already does, so no @font-face and no file in /fonts. Cost is about 25 KB of path data for the chosen face, against the ~6 KB single path today.
  • The J will come up. Friz's J drops 0.349 cap below the baseline and dies in the cut, so the word reads PRO|ECTS. Every sans here sits it on the baseline, so the morph raises the J into view. That changes the cut title's whole conceit and is worth deciding on purpose.
  • Tracking is per face. Each needs its own to span the measure — the table's Track column. That number replaces --cue-track's 0.02em at the sans end, so it has to be animated too, or baked into the target outlines as it is here.
